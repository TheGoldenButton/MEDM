Emoji Room — tap a topic for details
🎡 Emoji wheel

Press the index trigger on either controller (desktop: press L) to open the wheel — it opens in that hand and stays open; press again to close it and send. The golden ball rests in the centre. Point your other controller at it, hold its trigger (desktop: mouse button) and drag toward an emoji on the rim until the ball turns green, then let go to pick. Let go anywhere else and it returns to the centre. Closing the wheel sends your picks above your head for a few seconds; you can see it in the mirror.

🧚 Ask Spark

With the wheel closed, hold the index trigger on its own (desktop: hold L), speak your question, then let go. A quick tap still opens the wheel. A ball of light forms while you speak, then the fairy emerges and answers in emoji. It does not translate — it answers, using what you are looking at (in AR, the headset-camera view).

💙 Spark's child avatar

Walk up to the short blue avatar (Spark), face it, then send it some emoji — close your emoji wheel to send, or speak a voice translation. Spark receives your symbols plus what the avatar sees from its own eyes and replies as a young child in VR would. Watch: its own emoji wheel opens in front of it, its arm drags the golden ball to each symbol in turn — at a different speed every pick, sometimes eager, sometimes dawdling — its face becomes each emoji as it picks it (smile, frown, surprise, sleepy…), each pick plays that emoji's own single note on its wheel, pitched just like yours, with the same rising/falling shimmer when its wheel opens and closes, and the answer pops up above its head.

🎙️ Translate to emoji

Hold index + grip together on one controller with the wheel closed (desktop: hold V), or hold the trigger of the wheel hand while the wheel is open (desktop: hold L with the wheel open). Speak, then let go to send. Wave your free hand through the transcript box (desktop: Esc) to cancel.

📷 AR / VR

Tap AR Mode at the top (or the 📷 button in-headset) to switch between the virtual room and the real world through the headset cameras, without leaving the headset. Ask Spark uses that real-world view.

🕹️ Moving & pages

The left thumbstick (desktop: WASD, drag to look) walks you around, even with the wheel open. The right thumbstick left/right (desktop: Q/E or arrows) flips wheel pages, or snap-turns you when the wheel is closed. A (right controller) / Space (desktop) jumps. B / Y closes the wheel without sending. Hands only: make a fist to grab the air and pull yourself, like swimming — pull with one or both fists to glide that way, pull both fists down fast to jump, twist both fists like paddles to turn. Pinches are for the wheel and Spark.

🔍 Search

With the wheel open, point your other controller at the search box under the page name and pull its trigger (desktop: click it and type, Enter to search). Results appear on wheel page 2 and clear when the wheel closes.

⚙️ Settings & hands

In VR, point at ⚙️ at the top of your view, pull the trigger, then tap ✏️ to type your API key with the Meta keyboard (Meta Quest Browser 26.1+). With hand tracking, pinch index = trigger and pinch middle finger = grip. On desktop your right arm points wherever the mouse cursor points.

🙂 Face, eyes & hands

Your avatar's mouth moves as you talk (microphone lip-sync, with wide smiles on "ee"/"s" sounds and rounded lips on "oo"), it blinks, its eyes follow the wheel or fairy, and the brows and smile react to the emoji you send. If your headset exposes WebXR face tracking (e.g. Quest Pro) the real expression data takes over. Fingers curl with your trigger, grip and thumb. Turn the mic lip-sync off in ⚙️. Sound effects can be switched off and on there too.

Walk around the room and check yourself out in the mirror. WASD / left stick moves, drag looks around.

Settings — Meta / Muse APIs

One Meta API key is used for both Muse Voice Transcribe and Muse Spark. It is stored only in this browser (localStorage), never in this file. With a key: speech goes to Muse Voice Transcribe, the text goes to Muse Spark. Without a key, voice falls back to the headset's built-in speech recognition (the same engine as the keyboard's mic button, started automatically with no keyboard popping up) and Spark falls back to a local translator that turns your words into the best-matching emoji in word order, with redundant symbols pruned.

Spark receives one message: for translation, a short instruction to translate what you said into an emoji sequence of 12 or less; for Ask Spark (trigger-hold, wheel closed), an instruction to actually answer your question helpfully as a separate assistant, using the scene image for context. Lewd or hateful content is not translated.
